# A grant reaches the messages still waiting for it

Granting a contact **answers the messages they have just sent**. Precisely: a `consent.state.changed` whose subject is a contact and whose `new_state` is `granted` wakes each persona on every `inbound.message.received` that is still labelled `pending`, whose subject is that contact, that arrived on a connection the decision's own scope covers, and that arrived no longer than the **reach** before the decision was taken — one hour by default, the operator's to set, and refused, by the personas *and* by the Gateway, above the bus's duplicate window. Nothing else changes: **the envelope's label is not touched**, so [ADR 0012](0012-revoked-consent-reduces-publication.md)'s rule that the answer is stamped on a message at its arrival stands, and the SDK's consent gate goes on refusing everything that is not exactly `granted` — what admits a replayed message is a second, named question (`twalk_sdk.consent.GrantReach.reaches`) asked of the owner's own decision event, which is the only thing entitled to answer it. **The persona is the reader**, not a new component and not the Gateway: it follows the decision stream on a durable consumer created at the stream's *head* (`persona-<id>-decisions`), and on a grant opens an ephemeral consumer over the reach's window that acks nothing, re-reads the messages that are already there, and hands each to the same code path a live message goes through — the thinking event, the handler, the disclosure, the suggestion. So **nothing is re-published and nothing is re-stamped**: the trigger keeps its own id, its own `time` and its own `pending` label, the journal shows one message answered late rather than a contact who wrote twice, and the suggestion carries the contract's deterministic id on its first attempt, which is the id the live path would have produced. The reach is **`TWALK_GRANT_REACH_SECONDS`** on the persona, injected by the runtime from **`HERMES_GRANT_REACH_SECONDS`** ([ADR 0015](0015-no-default-llm-configured-through-the-companion.md)'s channel, never fetched by a persona), and read a third time by the Companion Gateway as **`GATEWAY_GRANT_REACH_SECONDS`** — because the Gateway is what tells the owner, *before* they decide, how many of that contact's messages a grant will answer: `GET /api/contacts/pending` carries `reach_seconds` and, on each row, a `waiting` counted from the bus per request and stored nowhere — keyed on the contact *and* the connection, because a grant is scoped and a row that showed the same person's mail beside their WhatsApp would promise what the decision does not do — and the consent screen says the number and names the window. A reach of `0` is a deployment that has turned the whole of this off, and the screen says that too. Only `pending` is reached, never `revoked`: ADR 0012 published a revoked sender's message without content, so granting them later cannot make it answerable — there is nothing in it to answer — and revocation therefore stays forward-only in both directions. Taken on 2026-09-27 against [#364](https://github.com/linagora/twalk/issues/364), which the owner opened after watching it fail on their own deployment.

Rationale: the timeline is the argument. On 2026-09-24 at 17:12:10Z a mail arrived from an address nobody had decided about, stamped `pending`; at 17:12:49Z the owner granted it. Thirty-nine seconds, and the message they had just watched arrive was the one message that would never be answered — no defect anywhere, every component doing exactly what it was told. That is not an edge case, it is **the common case, because a contact becomes interesting precisely because they just wrote**; and the Companion's own copy had made the defect into a promise, telling the owner in five languages to grant somebody and then *wait for their next message*. So the question was never whether a grant should reach backwards but **how far**, and the answer had to be small: reaching everything would turn a newly granted contact's whole history of pending mail into a queue of drafts nobody asked for, each one billed to a model. **An hour** is the suggestion policy's own number (`twalk_sdk.policy.DEFAULT_SUGGESTION_TTL_SECONDS`) and the same judgement — long enough that the owner gets to the screen after a meeting, short enough that granting somebody today does not answer what they wrote yesterday — and reusing it means a deployment has one idea of how long a message stays worth answering rather than two. The ceiling is not taste: the reach is **refused at startup above the bus's duplicate window** ([ADR 0037](0037-the-bus-keeps-ninety-days-and-two-gigabytes-and-no-more.md)'s day), and that refusal is what makes *"revoke, grant again, and the same message is not answered twice"* a property of the design rather than bookkeeping somebody has to maintain — inside the window a second replay recomputes the same `sha256(persona_id:trigger_event_id:attempt)` and the same thinking id, and the bus absorbs both, which is also why the **decision's own delivery is the unit of work**: a replay in which any message asks to be retried naks the decision and runs again whole, and the messages already answered cost nothing the second time. The replay lives in the persona because the alternatives are worse. Re-publishing the message with a new label is not merely inelegant, it is *impossible*: the event's id is its `Nats-Msg-Id`, so the republication is deduplicated away, and an id changed to get around that would be a second message from a contact who wrote once. A backfill writing suggestions directly would be a second producer of them, which is a second consent engine ([ADR 0006](0006-consent-state-owned-by-companion-gateway.md) says there is one). And a gate that consulted the consent snapshot on every message — asking "is this contact granted *now*?" instead of reading the label — would have been the smallest change and the wrong one: it makes every message's fate depend on a network call at processing time, it silently answers messages of any age, and it dissolves the one property the gate is documented to have, that it reads the envelope and nothing else. Finally, **the consent screen is the cap**, which is why there is no limit on how many drafts one replay may produce: the owner is shown the count for that contact before they click, so a grant that will answer nine messages is a promise they made knowingly — and a cap would have been a number nobody could explain in the one place it mattered. The count itself is a bus read rather than a stored figure for the reason `companion-gateway/src/contacts.rs` gives at length about display names: a per-message history of who wrote and when is the surveillance log that module exists to not become, and the probe it reads with deserialises five envelope attributes — the four the projection already reads, plus the label — and no `data` member at all. It is also **opt-in**, `?waiting=true`: the same endpoint is what the dashboard polls every fifteen seconds for a badge that does not draw the number, and a bus read on that tick would be a cost paid by a screen that has no use for it.

The costs are accepted rather than argued away. **One number lives in three variables**, and two that disagree make the consent screen lie — it would promise two messages and the persona would answer none, or the reverse. `deploy/docker-compose/compose.yaml` wires all three from one value and `deploy/README.md` says so, which is the same arrangement the suggestion window already has and no better than it. **A grant taken while no persona has ever run is missed**: the decisions consumer is created at the stream's head, deliberately — a persona installed today that replayed the owner's whole decision history would draft answers to every conversation ever granted — so the grant that reaches nothing is the one taken before the persona's first start, and `GrantReach.is_fresh` refuses any decision older than the reach for the same reason, which means a persona down for two hours comes back and does *not* answer what was granted while it was away. That is the right way round (answering yesterday's grant is the thing the reach exists to refuse) and it is a real hole: a deployment whose persona crashes at the wrong moment silently loses one replay, and the owner's remedy is the one they already have, which is to say something to the contact themselves. **A replay is N completions at once**, sequentially, on the owner's single click; the decision's delivery deadline is five minutes and the loop tells the bus it is still working after each message, but a contact who wrote a dozen times in the hour before the grant is a dozen billed completions and the screen said so first. **The count can under-report**: it reads at most five thousand messages of the window and says so in the log when it hits that, and a deployment whose bus carries more than that in an hour will show a number lower than what the persona then answers — the same bound, and the same asymmetry, that the persona's own read has. **`waiting: null` is not `0`** and a screen that rendered it as one would tell the owner nothing is waiting when the Gateway merely could not count; the Companion says nothing in that case, which is less useful and not misleading. **Several personas each answer the same replayed message**, exactly as they each answer a live one, so a deployment running two produces two drafts per reached message — unchanged behaviour, stated here because a replay makes it arrive all at once. And **the window is measured from the grant and not from now**, so a decision that took a while to reach a persona still answers the messages it was made about; the asymmetry with `is_fresh`, which measures from now, is deliberate. There is a **third clock**, and it is the one that can be seen: the Gateway counts what a grant taken *now* would answer, so a message at the very edge of the window can fall out of it while the owner reads the page, and the screen will have promised one more message than the persona answers. Recounting at the moment of the click and telling the owner afterwards would be exact and would also be a silence turned round the other way. **And the two sides do not place a connection identically**: the Gateway resolves an event's perimeter through the connection registry, as its projection must, while a persona has no registry and reads the envelope's own `connection`, falling back to the network the grant names — which the Gateway publishes on every decision alongside the connections, so the fallback holds for every decision this product produces. What is left is narrow and real: an event the registry cannot place is not counted and may still be reached, and an event published before `connection` existed is counted under the connection the registry placed it on while the persona judges it by its network. Both are deployments with a registry that does not describe them, which the projection already warns about once per cause.
